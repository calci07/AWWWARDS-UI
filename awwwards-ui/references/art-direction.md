# Art direction

Use this reference to turn a brief into a distinctive visual and experiential thesis.

## Contents

## Translate truth into form

Start with subject matter, not style labels. Extract:

- **Truth:** the most defensible or emotionally resonant fact about the brand, product, person, or institution.
- **Tension:** a useful contrast such as precision/humanity, heritage/future, abundance/restraint, urgency/control, or craft/scale.
- **Audience shift:** what the visitor should believe, feel, or do after the experience.
- **Evidence:** content that proves the proposition rather than merely claiming it.

Build concept routes from different truths or tensions. A valid route changes more than color and type; it changes composition, imagery, pacing, language, and interaction.

Use this formula:

> Express **[truth]** through **[metaphor]** so **[audience]** understands or feels **[shift]**.

Examples:

- Incident response: express coordinated calm through a signal resolving into a shared timeline.
- Architectural fragrance: express scent as structure through material planes, voids, and measured reveals.
- Motion portfolio: express editing rhythm through a living contact sheet and cinematic cuts.

## Select the route

Score each route from 1–5:

| Test | Question |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Could this only belong to this subject? |
| Ownability | Would it remain recognizable without the logo? |
| Narrative range | Can it sustain a full page without repetition? |
| Content fit | Does it make the real proof easier to understand? |
| Production fit | Can the team execute it well with available assets and time? |
| Resilience | Does the idea survive mobile, touch, slow devices, and reduced motion? |

Reject a visually exciting route that depends on missing content or cannot survive the constraints.

## Define the system

Create a compact direction sheet:

- **Concept sentence**
- **Brand adjectives:** three precise qualities; avoid empty terms such as premium or modern
- **Anti-adjectives:** three qualities to avoid
- **Composition grammar:** grid, dominant alignment, density, edge behavior, overlap, and whitespace
- **Type voice:** contrast, width, case, rhythm, and editorial role
- **Color logic:** what is structural, atmospheric, semantic, and interactive
- **Image logic:** subject, crop, depth, texture, treatment, and repetition rules
- **Motion verbs:** two to four verbs such as cut, assemble, track, fold, resolve, drift, or snap
- **Signature motif:** one memorable behavior tied to the concept
- **Quiet mode:** how supporting sections create contrast

## Compose with intent

Use layout to control attention:

- Establish a dominant axis, then break it deliberately.
- Create contrast through scale, density, and pacing before adding decoration.
- Use negative space as active structure, not leftover space.
- Let type and imagery share a spatial rule.
- Repeat a motif with variation; do not repeat a template unchanged.
- Keep essential reading order obvious even when the composition is asymmetric.
- Design mobile as a re-authored composition with the same thesis.

Avoid the automatic “creative website” package: centered slogan, glowing gradient blob, glass cards, floating orb, endless marquee, custom cursor, and scroll-pinned panels. Any one of these can work when derived from the concept; the package rarely is.

## Make content carry the concept

Map sections to visitor questions rather than common landing-page labels:

| Visitor question | Useful evidence |
|---|---|
| What is this? | Precise proposition, product or work shown immediately |
| Why should I care? | Outcome, tension, sensory promise, or cultural relevance |
| Why believe it? | Product evidence, process, metrics, provenance, or case work |
| Is it for me? | Use cases, audience language, fit, constraints, or examples |
| What happens next? | Clear action, commitment level, reassurance, and alternatives |

Write or request the proof before decorating empty sections. Never invent evidence.

## Research references

When references are needed, collect them by problem:

- typography or editorial rhythm;
- image treatment or art direction;
- navigation and information architecture;
- motion grammar;
- material or spatial behavior;
- mobile reinterpretation.

Analyze why each reference works. Do not copy a site’s surface package. Combine insights from different domains so the result is transformed and specific.

## Originality review

Remove or redesign an element when:

- it could be transferred unchanged to unrelated brands;
- it echoes the inspiration more strongly than the subject;
- it exists only because award-gallery sites often use it;
- it needs an explanation to justify its relevance;
- it weakens the primary action or proof;
- its fallback loses essential meaning.

A strong result can be quiet. Novelty is a relationship between idea, content, and execution—not the number of effects.
